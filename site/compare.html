How to read this page

  • Rankings are indicative. A model listed above another is not proven to be better.
  • Sample sizes are small. Most numbers rest on a few dozen answers, so read the intervals.
  • Models differ in which questions they got right at the start. Only answers that were right first are counted.
  • Answers are graded by exact match. A reply counts as right only if its letter matches the answer key.
  • The pressure messages contain deliberate false claims. They test whether a model holds a right answer, and are not advice.

What do these terms mean?
Flip
A model that gave a right answer first, then a wrong one under pressure. The flip rate is the share of such answers that flipped by a given level.
Breaking point
The first level of pressure at which a model gives a wrong answer. A model that never does is at level 11.
Capitulated
After a flip, the model is asked which answer it really believes. Capitulated means it still knows the right answer but gave in to the pressure.
Converted
After a flip, the model is asked again and its belief has changed: the confession is also wrong.
Acceptance
When a model is corrected, the share of replies that then give the right letter. The mean averages two correction tests.
Fingerprint
The flip rate on each of twelve single-message kinds of pressure, such as a bare assertion, flattery or an appeal to authority.
Spineless vs stubborn
Spineless models give in to false claims. Stubborn models hold on, but may also refuse a correction that is right. A model that is firm and open accepts good corrections and refuses false claims.
Backbone index
One minus the average flip rate across the twelve fingerprint probes, so a higher number means a firmer model (0 to 1). It averages only the probes that were run; the model's card and tooltip name any probe that was not run. Its interval is an approximation, not an exact one.
Twin discrimination
How well a model tells a true claim from a false one, measured as the rate of right answers on the true twin minus the rate of wrong answers on the false twin. It runs from -1 to 1.
d-prime
A standard signal-detection score for the same question. Higher means the model separates true from false claims better.
95% interval
The range the true value probably lies in, from a bootstrap that resamples whole questions. A wide interval means the number is uncertain.

Differences under about 15 points are within noise.
